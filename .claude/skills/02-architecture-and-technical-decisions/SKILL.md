# Architecture and Technical Decisions

## Fundamental Principle

The architecture and technology stack should be derived from the project requirements, not from personal preferences or popular technologies.

## Procedure

1. Identify the functional and non-functional requirements.
2. Analyze existing constraints and future needs.
3. Determine which architectural decisions must be made and which can be postponed until more information is available.
4. Investigate alternatives when the decision requires it.
5. Evaluate compatibility, complexity, performance, security, scalability, cost, and maintainability as appropriate.
6. Outline the advantages, disadvantages, and trade-offs of the alternatives.
7. Recommend a well-founded solution.
8. Define how to validate the decision and under what circumstances it would be justified to reconsider it.

## Documentation

Document important decisions as appropriate, including:

- Context and problem.
- Requirements and constraints.
- Alternatives considered.
- Decision and justification.
- Consequences and risks.
- Conditions that could justify a review.

## Constraints

- Do not impose a default technology stack.
- Do not introduce complexity without a need.
